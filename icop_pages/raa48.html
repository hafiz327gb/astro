When to Observe Rabee' Al-Awwal Waxing (NEW) Crescent ?


The geocentric conjunction (Geocentric New Moon) will occur Inshalla on (Wednesday 12 August 2026) at 17:37 UT.

Sighting the new crescent on (Wednesday 12 August 2026) and (Thursday 13 August 2026) is shown in the below graphs using the program Accurate Times by Mohammad Odeh according to Odeh criterion. Where:-

  • It is impossible to see the crescent from the areas located under the red color. Because either the Moon on this day sets before the Sunset and/or the topocentric conjunction occurs after the Sunset.
  • The crescent is expected to be seen by optical aid only from the areas located under the blue color.
  • The crescent is expected to be seen by optical aid from the areas located under the magenta color. In these areas the crescent could be seen by naked eye if the atmospheric conditions are superb and the observer is experienced.
  • The crescent is expected to be easily visible by naked eye from the areas located under the green color.
  • The crescent cannot be seen from uncolored areas, even though the Moon sets in these locations after the Sunset and the topocentric conjunction occurs before the Sunset, but the Moon is not sufficiently illuminated in order to be seen as crescent even by optical aid.
  • Kindly notice that the below graph shows the possibility of seeing the crescent from areas between 60 degrees north of Equator down to 60 degrees south of Equator.

According to the Universal Hejric Calendar (UHC), which is based on the calculated crescent visibility, the start of this month in the Eastern Region will be on Friday 14 August 2026 and in the Western Region will be on Friday 14 August 2026. Kindly notice that the UHC is a pre-calculated calendar, which adopts a certain criterion to start the new Hejric month. Your country/organization might adopt different criterion to start the new Hejric month. So it is highly advised to read the UHC website before giving any judgment.

1 . Time of observation: After sunset. Not Seen: ICOP member Mr. Hilal France from Pau City in Nouvelle-Aquitaine State mentioned that the sky was clear, the atmospheric condition was superb, the crescent was not seen by naked eye, the crescent was not seen by binocular, the crescent was not seen by telescope, the crescent was not sought by CCD Imaging 
Mr. Hilal France said: "Observation Report — Lunar Crescent of Rabi' al-Awwal 1448, Thursday, August 13, 2026 (France)

The lunar crescent was not observed anywhere in metropolitan France, despite the deployment of teams spread across the entire territory. This included trained referent observers equipped with high-performance gear: Celestron 20×80 binoculars and a Celestron 127 SLT telescope on a GoTo mount.
Astronomical Context

The lunar conjunction took place the day before, on Wednesday, August 12, 2026, at 17:37 (UT), materialized by the partial solar eclipse visible from all of metropolitan France.
On the evening of August 13, the Moon was 24 hours and 44 minutes old since the conjunction: the crescent remained extremely thin and very low on the western horizon.
The teams deployed to sites carefully chosen for their clear western horizons, an essential condition for searching for such a low crescent.
Astronomical Data (calculated for Serres-Castet, Pyrenees-Atlantiques)

At sunset on this Thursday, August 13, 2026, the Moon was 24 hours and 44 minutes old since the conjunction. The Sun set at 21:10 and the Moon 27 minutes later, at 21:37. This interval between sunset and moonset, known as the "lag time," was therefore only 27 minutes, close to all-time records, leaving only a very narrow observation window before the Moon also disappeared below the horizon. The crescent was only 1.45% illuminated (Moon-Sun elongation of 13.8°). Most importantly, the Moon was already very low at sunset, at approximately +3.3° above the horizon (azimuth 278°, due west); and at the most favorable moment for observation, around 21:22, it was only at +1.2°, on the verge of setting itself.
Global Visibility Map

The predicted crescent visibility map (Odeh method) shows that France was divided into two zones:

    The northern part of the country was in the red "crescent not visible" zone: the hilal was detectable neither with the naked eye nor with an instrument.

    The central-southern part fell into the "visible with optical aid" zone: the crescent was potentially accessible there, but only with binoculars or a telescope, and never with the naked eye.

Nowhere in France was the crescent visible to the naked eye that evening. This map confirms, on a global scale, what our observers noted in the field.
Observation Conditions

The evening took place in the middle of summer, under intense heat and during the holiday season, which did not prevent a strong turnout. The sky was largely clear over most of France at sunset: only the southeast quarter (Alpine relief, Lyon area) and some scattered cloudy spells along the Pyrenees presented cloud cover. Observation conditions were therefore, this time, generally good across the vast majority of locations.

The non-observation is therefore not explained by the weather, but by the very low altitude of the Moon and an exceptionally short lag time: the Moon, already very low on the horizon at sunset, set barely 27 minutes after it: a value that places this configuration very close to world lag time records. So low on the horizon and immersed in the last glimmer of twilight, the crescent, faintly illuminated (1.45%), remained out of reach to the naked eye and, at best, at the limit of detectability with an instrument.

Training and Transmission

This evening was also a time for training and transmission:

    The day before, during the solar eclipse on August 12, a Hilal France team had gone to Labastide-Cezeracq (Pyrenees-Atlantiques) alongside residents of Pau, to share this astronomical moment with them.

    The following day, for the hilal, Hilal France organized a theoretical training on the crescent for the worshippers of the Pau mosque, held at the Pau mosque, followed by a practical field observation training in Serres-Castet (Pyrenees-Atlantiques).

    Several new referent observers, who were still regular group members the day before, were deployed in the field, notably at Puy Loup (63).

Conclusion and Announcement

The lunar crescent of Rabi' al-Awwal 1448 was not observed on Thursday, August 13, 2026, in France, due to the very low altitude of the crescent on the horizon and a very short lag time, with the Moon setting barely 27 minutes after the Sun, very close to world lag time records.

Consequently, Hilal France announces that the month of Safar 1448 will have 30 days and that the 1st day of Rabi' al-Awwal 1448 will be on Saturday, August 15, 2026, insha'Allah.

May Allah greatly reward all the observers mobilized despite the heat and the holiday period, and accept the effort of all those who keep this Sunnah alive.

When to Observe Safar Waning (OLD) Crescent ?


The geocentric conjunction (Geocentric New Moon) will occur Inshalla on (Wednesday 12 August 2026) at 17:37 UT.

Sighting the OLD crescent on (Wednesday 12 August 2026) and (Tuesday 11 August 2026) is shown in the below graphs using the program Accurate Times by Mohammad Odeh according to Odeh criterion. Where:-

  • It is impossible to see the OLD crescent from the areas located under the red color. Because either the Moon on this day rises after the Sunrise and/or the topocentric conjunction occurs before the Sunrise.
  • The crescent is expected to be seen by optical aid only from the areas located under the blue color.
  • The crescent is expected to be seen by optical aid from the areas located under the magenta color. In these areas the crescent could be seen by naked eye if the atmospheric conditions are superb and the observer is experienced.
  • The crescent is expected to be easily visible by naked eye from the areas located under the green color.
  • The crescent cannot be seen from uncolored areas, even though the Moon rises in these locations before the Sunrise and the topocentric conjunction occurs after the Sunrise, but the Moon is not sufficiently illuminated in order to be seen as crescent even by optical aid.
